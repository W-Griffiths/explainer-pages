02Architecture

Separate, but connected

The systems the business runs on, such as Vantagepoint, ADP and SharePoint, stay on their own. Every app keeps its own data in its own database, and reads what it needs from each of them.

Ready for AI. MCP, the open standard for connecting AI tools to data, can give AI assistants the same secure access. It's backed by Microsoft, Google and OpenAI.
“Two services shouldn't share a data store. Each service manages its own private data store.”
Microsoft's architecture guidance
One source of truthData stays where it lives

Data from Vantagepoint, ADP or SharePoint stays where it is, and apps read it live rather than keeping copies. Each app's own data lives only in its own database, so every piece of data has exactly one home.

No noisy neighborsBusy apps stay out of the way

Microsoft's name for the risk of sharing: “the activity of one tenant can negatively affect another.” With separate databases, each app's own work runs on its own server, away from the systems the business depends on.

Least privilegeOnly the access each app needs

NIST, the US standards agency: “restrict the access privileges … to the minimum necessary to accomplish assigned tasks.” Each app's login reaches only its own database.

Connected by designOne app, many databases

An app can connect to several databases at once: “clients can open multiple connections to the same database, or different databases.”

The detail

Separate databases

A database server is the software, and usually the machine, that stores data. A database is one app's set of tables inside it. One server can hold many databases, each kept apart from the others.

Microsoft's guidance is that each service should own its data: “Each service manages its own private data store.” When apps share one, “If the data schema changes, the change must be coordinated across every service that relies on that database.”

Apps “can safely share the same physical database server” as long as each keeps its own data, which is how our PostgreSQL server works. But “sharing a single resource inherently carries the risk of noisy neighbor problems that you can't completely avoid,” which is why the systems the business relies on most are kept on their own.

Apps still use the data in Vantagepoint and our other systems. Each opens a separate connection and reads only the records it needs, such as projects and employees, at the moment it needs them.

The tools built around PostgreSQL

Modern web apps talk to their database through toolkits that handle the details. PostgreSQL is the one they're designed around:

  • Prisma: its next major version, due for general release in October 2026, launches with PostgreSQL first, and “Today every extension that adds column types, operators, or index types targets PostgreSQL.” In the current version, several features are marked “PostgreSQL only.”
  • node-postgres: the standard PostgreSQL connector for Node.js was downloaded 39.5 million times in one week of September 2026.
  • Add-ons: PostgreSQL “is highly extensible.” PostGIS adds “storing, indexing, and querying geospatial data” , and pgvector adds AI similarity search.
More on PostgreSQL

PostgreSQL started at the University of California, Berkeley in 1986 and has been developed in the open ever since. Each major version is supported “for 5 years after its initial release,” with fixes at least every three months.

Share of developers using PostgreSQL in Stack Overflow's survey: 32.9% in 2018, 36.1% in 2020, 43.6% in 2022, 45.6% in 2023 (when it became #1), 48.7% in 2024 and 55.6% in 2025. It has also been the most admired and most wanted database every year since 2023. JetBrains' 2025 survey of 24,534 developers also ranks it first. DB-Engines has named it database of the year four times, more than any other.

Microsoft is one of the project's biggest contributors: 19 of its employees work on PostgreSQL , and 14.2% of the changes in the next release have Microsoft authors. Its head of Azure databases says “PostgreSQL has become foundational to how modern applications are built.”

Licensing and cost

The PostgreSQL License is approved by the Open Source Initiative , and the project says “there are no plans to change the PostgreSQL License.”

SQL Server 2025 list prices, per 2-core pack: Enterprise $15,123, Standard $3,945. Microsoft requires “a minimum of four licenses per virtual OSE” (each virtual machine), and “a container is also considered a virtual OSE.”

The cost chart shows one server or container at that four-core minimum: 2 packs × $3,945 for Standard, and 2 packs × $15,123 for Enterprise.
